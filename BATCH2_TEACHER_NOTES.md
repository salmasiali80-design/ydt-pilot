# VOCABULARY EXPLANATIONS

## Q1 — Correct Answer: B) validity

**Stem signal:**
"assessment procedures" + "concerns about" = Can we trust the assessment? Is it measuring what it should?

**Why B is correct:**
**validity** = the quality of being logically sound and measuring what it is supposed to measure. In education and testing, "validity of assessment" is a strong, fixed collocation.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) rigidity = katılık, esnek olmama
- B) validity = geçerlilik ✅
- C) scarcity = kıtlık, azlık
- D) liability = sorumluluk / yükümlülük / risk
- E) intensity = yoğunluk, şiddet

**Teaching reflex:**
When students see **assessment / test / procedure / measurement**, train them to think immediately of:
> validity · reliability · accuracy · objectivity · fairness

## Q2 — Correct Answer: C) adaptation

**Stem signal:**
"enables them to survive" = survival mechanism in a hostile environment.

**Why C is correct:**
**adaptation** = a biological feature or behaviour that helps an organism survive in a particular environment. The appositive structure "a biological ----" signals a scientific noun.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) depletion = tükenme, azalması
- B) inference = çıkarım
- C) adaptation = uyum / adaptasyon ✅
- D) exclusion = dışlama
- E) deficiency = eksiklik, yetersizlik

**Teaching reflex:**
In biology/ecology stems, phrases like **survive · extreme climates · drought · environment · species** point to:
> adaptation · resilience · mechanism · strategy

**Stem signal:**
"inscriptions" + "ancient languages" + "texts" = decode or read old, difficult writing.

**Why D is correct:**
**decipher** = to succeed in reading or understanding something coded, ancient, or difficult to interpret.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) suppress = bastırmak, engellemek
- B) prescribe = reçete etmek / kural olarak belirtmek
- C) distort = çarpıtmak

For **ancient inscriptions · manuscripts · scripts · hieroglyphics**, the exam favours:

## Q4 — Correct Answer: E) curtailed

**Stem signal:**
"stricter safety regulations" → accidents must go DOWN, not up.

**Why E is correct:**
**curtail** = to reduce, limit, or cut down something undesirable. Direction of meaning is essential here.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) prolonged = uzatmak
- B) intensified = yoğunlaştırmak, artırmak
- C) facilitated = kolaylaştırmak
- D) displaced = yerinden etmek / yerini almak
- E) curtailed = azaltmak, sınırlamak ✅

**Trap alert:**
B) intensified and C) facilitated are opposite-direction traps. Students who miss the logic of "stricter regulations" fall into these.

**Teaching reflex:**
When the object is **accidents · risks · damage · pollution · spread of disease**, look for:
> reduce · limit · curtail · prevent · minimise

With **understanding / interpretation / analysis / perception**, strong positive adjectives include:
> nuanced · comprehensive · sophisticated · accurate · precise

## Q6 — Correct Answer: C) prompted

**Stem signal:**
"discovery has ---- renewed debate" = caused / triggered / initiated debate.

**Why C is correct:**
**prompt** = to cause or trigger something, especially a reaction, discussion, or response. Very common in academic and exam texts.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) restrained = sınırlamak, dizginlemek

## Q7 — Correct Answer: B) indispensable

**Stem signal:**
"Although difficult at first" + "eventually become" + "drought-affected regions" = essential, cannot be done without.

**Why B is correct:**
**indispensable** = absolutely necessary; impossible to do without. The concessive structure "although...at first / eventually" signals transformation from difficult → essential.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) incidental = tesadüfi / ikincil önemde
- B) indispensable = vazgeçilmez ✅
- C) contentious = tartışmalı
- D) provisional = geçici
- E) negligible = önemsiz, ihmal edilebilir

**Trap alert:**
D) provisional (temporary) is a near-opposite trap. E) negligible (unimportant) is a complete reversal of the intended meaning.

**Teaching reflex:**
When a sentence shows something that begins with difficulty but becomes critical in a serious context, expect:
> essential · indispensable · vital · crucial · imperative

## Q8 — Correct Answer: D) fragmentary

**Stem signal:**
"too ---- to support a definitive conclusion" = evidence is incomplete or insufficient.

**Why D is correct:**
**fragmentary** = incomplete, consisting of small disconnected pieces. The "too...to" structure always signals a negative/limiting quality.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) coherent = tutarlı
- B) persuasive = ikna edici
- C) conclusive = kesin sonuç veren
- D) fragmentary = parçalı, eksik ✅
- E) measurable = ölçülebilir

**Trap alert:**
C) conclusive is the perfect opposite trap — students who read carelessly may choose it because it relates to "conclusion."

**Teaching reflex:**
The phrase **"too ---- to support a conclusion"** always needs a negative/insufficient adjective:
> limited · insufficient · fragmentary · inconclusive · sparse

**Stem signal:**
"not because economically profitable, BUT because connected to cultural identity" = deeply / essentially / by its very nature connected.

**Why E is correct:**
**intrinsically** = naturally, essentially, by its very nature. The "not...but" contrast structure isolates the true reason: the crafts survive because of their essential cultural connection.

**Choice meanings:**
- A) marginally = çok az, sınırlı ölçüde
- B) arbitrarily = keyfi olarak
- C) reluctantly = isteksizce
- D) superficially = yüzeysel olarak
- E) intrinsically = özünde, doğası gereği ✅

**Trap alert:**
D) superficially is the perfect opposite trap — a surface connection vs an essential one.

**Teaching reflex:**
With **identity · memory · culture · value · meaning**, the exam tests adverbs of depth:
> deeply · closely · intrinsically · historically · fundamentally

**Stem signal:**
"transportation system designed to ---- movement" = make movement more efficient, faster, smoother.

**Why A is correct:**
**streamline** = to make a process, system, or movement simpler, faster, and more efficient.

**Choice meanings:**

> streamline · facilitate · enhance · optimise · improve

# GRAMMAR EXPLANATIONS

## Q11 — Correct Answer: A) was discovered / had disappeared

**Stem signal:**
"By the time" = one past action happens BEFORE another past action.

**Logic:**
Settlement was discovered in the early 20th century.
BEFORE that → wooden structures had already disappeared.

> By the time it **was discovered** → structures **had disappeared**

**Why not B?**
"had been discovered / disappeared" reverses the sequence. It implies discovery happened first — which contradicts the intended meaning.

**Teaching reflex:**
> **By the time + simple past** → the other clause usually needs **past perfect (had + V3)**

---

## Q12 — Correct Answer: C) had vanished / were introduced

**Stem signal:**
"before" = two events in the past; one happened earlier than the other.

**Logic:**
Species disappeared FIRST.
Conservation measures came LATER.

> Species **had vanished** before measures **were introduced**.

**Why passive in the second blank?**
Measures do not introduce themselves — they are introduced by authorities. Passive is grammatically required.

**Trap alert:**
D) "were vanished" — **vanish** is an intransitive verb and cannot be passivised. This is a classic Turkish learner error. Train students to recognise intransitive verbs.

**Teaching reflex:**
> With **before + past event**: earlier action = **past perfect** / later action = **simple past**

---

## Q13 — Correct Answer: D) preserved

**Stem signal:**
"The manuscripts, ---- in a remote monastery for centuries" = which WERE preserved (passive, completed state).

**Logic:**
Reduced passive relative clause:
> The manuscripts, **which were preserved** → **preserved** (past participle only)

**Why not E) being preserved?**
"Being preserved" = currently in the process of being preserved. But "for centuries" + the main clause "are now being digitised" shows the preservation is a completed/historical passive state, not an ongoing one.

**Teaching reflex:**
> Passive adjective clause reduction: **which was/were + V3 → V3**

---

## Q14 — Correct Answer: E) to solve

**Stem signal:**
"The ability ----" = fixed structure requires to-infinitive.

**Logic:**
> the ability **to solve** complex problems

**Why not D) to have solved?**
Perfect infinitive suggests a completed prior action — unnecessary and illogical in this context.

**Teaching reflex:**
Memorise as exam patterns:
> ability / capacity / tendency / willingness / attempt / decision + **to V1**

---

## Q15 — Correct Answer: A) reading

**Stem signal:**
"avoid ----" = gerund follows avoid, always.

**Logic:**
> avoid **reading**

**Why not B) to read?**
"Avoid to read" is grammatically wrong in English.

**Teaching reflex:**
High-frequency gerund verbs — memorise as chunks:
> avoid · consider · deny · enjoy · finish · postpone · practise · risk · suggest + **V-ing**

---

## Q16 — Correct Answer: C) developed

**Stem signal:**
"The vaccine, ---- after years of research" = which WAS developed (passive, completed).

**Logic:**
Reduced passive relative clause:
> The vaccine, **which was developed** → **developed**

**Why not E) being developed?**
"Being developed" implies the vaccine is still under development. But "has helped reduce the spread" proves it is already in use — development is complete.

**Teaching reflex:**
> If the noun already produces results, the reduced clause shows a completed passive:
> developed · discovered · built · designed · introduced · established

---

## Q17 — Correct Answer: E) whom

**Stem signal:**
"many of ----" referring to visitors (people) + preposition "of" before the relative pronoun.

**Logic:**
> many of **whom** are interested…

**Why not D) that?**
After a preposition, **that** cannot be used as a relative pronoun.
> ❌ many of that / ✅ many of whom

**Teaching reflex:**
> some / many / most / all / none + of + **whom** (people) / **which** (things)

---

## Q18 — Correct Answer: B) associated with

**Stem signal:**
"events ---- strong emotional responses" = which are associated WITH.

**Logic:**
Reduced passive relative clause + fixed preposition:
> events **which are associated with** → **associated with**

**Trap alert:**
All other options use wrong prepositions: into / to / from / by. The collocation is exclusively **associated WITH**.

**Teaching reflex:**
Fixed preposition collocations to memorise:
> associated **with** · related **to** · linked **to/with** · connected **to/with**

---

## Q19 — Correct Answer: D) making

**Stem signal:**
"postponed ----" = gerund follows postpone, always.

**Logic:**
> postponed **making** a final decision

**Why not A) to make?**
"Postponed to make" is grammatically wrong.

**Teaching reflex:**
Teach as a vocabulary chunk:
> postpone making a decision
> avoid making a mistake
> consider taking action
> deny causing damage

---

## Q20 — Correct Answer: E) to have been built

**Stem signal:**
"is believed ----" + past time reference + passive action = perfect passive infinitive required.

**Logic:**
The temple was built in the past (late Roman period).
The belief exists now.
The temple did not build itself → passive.

> is believed **to have been built**

**Why not B) to build?**
Active meaning: the temple built something — impossible.

**Why not A) being built?**
Implies current construction — contradicts "late Roman period."

**Teaching reflex:**
Critical YDT pattern — memorise as a template:
> is believed / thought / known / said / reported + **to have been V3**

Examples:
> The temple is believed **to have been built**…
> The manuscript is thought **to have been written**…
> The city is said **to have been founded**…
